Six months of AI-generated buy signals on my own portfolio. Up 13.8%.

That number is the least interesting thing in the write-up, for four
reasons — it's return on capital deployed rather than time-weighted, the
market was up too, N=1 with no control, and six months is noise.

Here's the part that actually taught me something.

44% of the gain came from one position that was never in my target
allocation. It surfaced on the watch list, came back a STRONG BUY at 88%
confidence with an RSI of 18.6, and I bought it off-plan — exactly the
decision allocation targets exist to prevent.

The tool told me to set a limit at $370. I did, and it filled.

It stayed a STRONG BUY for three days running — 88%, 88%, 91% — cheaper each
day. On the third one the same feature named a different price: $360,
reasoning from the 90-day consolidation floor. It traded to $349.20 shortly
after.

Put the two numbers side by side and the flaw is hard to unsee. The second is
anchored to where the stock had actually spent 90 days. The first is anchored
to the spot price — a small buffer below wherever it happened to be trading
that morning, described in the vocabulary of support. That cost about four
percentage points, and I got there by following the instructions.

If you build something like this for yourself: a suggested number gets
exactly as much credibility as the thing it's anchored to, and a model will
narrate a spot-price offset in the same confident register it uses for real
support. One card can't show you that. Two, two days apart, can.
