I applied for LinkedIn API access, waited two weeks, and got rejected for a
reason I hadn't considered. Posting about it here, which I appreciate is a
little on the nose.

The rejection said nothing about my code, my privacy policy, or what the app
does. It came down to one line:

"Provide a valid link to your organizational website."

I'd given them the project's GitHub Pages docs site. Not accepted. What the
review wants is evidence of an organisation, not evidence of a project — and
that's a fair rule for a product whose permission is literally called
w_organization_social. My honest answer to "is there an organisation here" is
no. There's a repo and one user.

So I could buy a domain and build a landing page purely to pass a review, or
leave the integration dormant. I left it dormant.

The estimate I got wrong is the transferable part. I scoped four social
integrations as engineering work. The code was hours each. Platform access
was weeks, and two of the four failed on criteria no amount of code could
satisfy — one on price, one on corporate identity.

If you're planning this for a side project: budget the approvals, not the
integrations.
